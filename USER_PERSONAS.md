# Soft Calm 🌸 - User Personas

## Persona 1: Maya Chen
### "The Overwhelmed Professional"

**Demographics:**
- Age: 28
- Location: Seattle, WA
- Occupation: Software Developer
- Education: Bachelor's in Computer Science
- Living Situation: Lives alone in a studio apartment

**Background:**
Maya was diagnosed with Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) at age 24. She excels at her job but struggles with the sensory demands of modern life—bright office lights, open-plan workspaces, notification overload, and crowded public transit. She experiences sensory meltdowns 2-3 times per month, which leave her exhausted and unable to work for days.

**Sensory Sensitivities:**
- **Visual:** Sensitive to bright lights, high contrast interfaces, busy patterns, and rapid animations
- **Auditory:** Overwhelmed by sudden sounds, background noise, and notification pings
- **Cognitive:** Struggles with cluttered interfaces and unpredictable navigation patterns

**Goals:**
- Track daily mood and identify sensory triggers
- Find calming activities during overwhelming moments
- Build a routine that accommodates her sensory needs
- Reduce frequency and intensity of meltdowns

**Frustrations:**
- Most mental health apps use bright colors and flashy animations that trigger her sensitivities
- Complex navigation and hidden features increase cognitive load
- Apps that don't remember her preferences across sessions
- Sudden pop-ups and notifications that startle her

**Technology Comfort Level:** High
- Uses smartphone daily for work and personal tasks
- Prefers apps with clear, predictable interfaces
- Has accessibility features enabled (reduce motion, dark mode) on all devices

**Ideal Experience with Soft Calm:**
Maya wants an app that "just gets it"—one that respects her sensory needs from the first interaction. She values the Sensory Comfort Mode slider because it lets her adjust the app's intensity based on her current state. On good days, she can handle more visual information; on difficult days, she can dial everything down. She needs her preferences saved so she never has to re-configure settings during a moment of overwhelm.

**Quote:**
*"I need an app that doesn't become another source of sensory stress. Everything else in my life demands so much—I just want one space that feels safe."*

## Persona 2: James Rodriguez
### "The Anxious Parent"

**Demographics:**
- Age: 35
- Location: Austin, TX
- Occupation: Elementary School Teacher
- Education: Master's in Education
- Living Situation: Married with two children (ages 6 and 9)

**Background:**
James has Sensory Processing Disorder (SPD) and Generalized Anxiety Disorder. His conditions went undiagnosed until his oldest child was evaluated for similar challenges. James finds it difficult to manage the sensory chaos of a classroom while maintaining his composure. Crowded spaces, loud environments, and unexpected changes to routine trigger intense anxiety and physical discomfort.

**Sensory Sensitivities:**
- **Visual:** Dislikes cluttered environments, fluorescent lighting, and busy interfaces
- **Auditory:** Sensitive to loud voices, sudden sounds, and overlapping conversations
- **Tactile:** Uncomfortable with certain textures and temperatures
- **Vestibular:** Gets overwhelmed in crowded, fast-moving environments

**Goals:**
- Develop coping strategies for sensory overwhelm at work
- Practice breathing exercises and grounding techniques during breaks
- Track which situations trigger sensory discomfort
- Share progress with his therapist
- Model healthy coping for his children who have similar sensitivities

**Frustrations:**
- Meditation apps with too many options and complex menus
- Apps that require reading long instructions or tutorials
- Being forced to create accounts before exploring features
- Apps that don't work offline (school has spotty WiFi)
- Forgetting passwords and complicated recovery processes

**Technology Comfort Level:** Medium
- Comfortable with basic smartphone functions
- Prefers simple, intuitive interfaces
- Appreciates visual cues (icons, emojis) over text-heavy instructions
- Struggles with apps that require frequent updates or maintenance

**Ideal Experience with Soft Calm:**
James needs an app he can open during his 15-minute breaks without spending half that time navigating menus. The predictable navigation with one main action per screen is perfect for his cognitive load during stressful moments. He values the visible back button because getting "lost" in an app increases his anxiety. The ability to quickly access breathing exercises or calming sounds without multiple taps is essential.

**Quote:**
*"When I'm overwhelmed, I can't handle complicated. I need simple, gentle, and fast—something that helps me calm down, not figure things out."*

## Persona 3: Aisha Osman
### "The Cautious Survivor"

**Demographics:**
- Age: 42
- Location: Minneapolis, MN
- Occupation: Freelance Graphic Designer
- Education: Associate's Degree in Visual Arts
- Living Situation: Lives with her sister and niece

**Background:**
Aisha has Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder (PTSD) from a traumatic event three years ago. She experiences hypervigilance, startle responses, and sensory sensitivities that weren't present before. Sudden sounds, bright flashing lights, and crowded spaces can trigger panic attacks. She's been working with a therapist and is slowly rebuilding her sense of safety and control in the world.

**Sensory Sensitivities:**
- **Visual:** Triggered by flashing lights, sudden movements, and high-contrast patterns
- **Auditory:** Extremely sensitive to sudden or unexpected sounds
- **Emotional:** Avoids content that feels chaotic, aggressive, or unpredictable
- **Startle Response:** Easily startled by notifications, animations, or pop-ups

**Goals:**
- Create a daily routine that feels safe and predictable
- Track trauma triggers and recovery progress
- Practice grounding techniques to manage panic attacks
- Build confidence in managing her sensory environment
- Maintain privacy—doesn't want others knowing about her condition

**Frustrations:**
- Apps with autoplay videos or sounds that startle her
- Bright splash screens or loading animations
- Apps that share data or show ads
- Complex privacy settings buried in menus
- Social features that pressure her to connect with others
- Losing access to her data if she forgets login credentials

**Technology Comfort Level:** Medium-High
- Professional designer, understands good vs. bad UX
- Privacy-conscious—reads terms of service and permission requests
- Appreciates thoughtful, trauma-informed design
- Needs reassurance that her data is secure and private

**Ideal Experience with Soft Calm:**
Aisha needs to feel in complete control of her experience. The Sensory Comfort Mode slider gives her that control—she can minimize visual intensity on days when she's feeling triggered. She values that the app has no ads, pop-ups, or social features that might surprise her. The authentication flows need to feel secure but not complicated. She wants to know her mood data and preferences are safely stored and accessible only to her, especially since she shares devices with family occasionally.

**Quote:**
*"After everything I've been through, I need to know I'm safe—even in the apps I use. Soft Calm should be my sanctuary, not another thing I have to defend myself against."*

## Common Themes Across Personas

**Shared Needs:**
1. **Control:** All users need to feel in control of their sensory environment
2. **Predictability:** Consistent, simple navigation reduces cognitive load and anxiety
3. **Gentleness:** Soft colors, generous spacing, and no sudden changes are essential
4. **Privacy:** Users want their sensitive health data protected and not shared
5. **Accessibility:** Large text, clear icons, and intuitive interactions are crucial
6. **Reliability:** Preferences must persist across sessions and devices

**Design Implications:**
- The Sensory Comfort Mode slider addresses the core need for control
- One action per screen with visible back buttons reduces overwhelm
- Soft pastel 3D-shaded colors create a calming, gentle aesthetic
- Complete authentication with password recovery builds trust and security
- Supabase integration ensures preferences and data sync across devices
- No ads, pop-ups, or social pressure respects users' need for safety

**Success Metrics:**
- Users report feeling calmer after using the app
- Users successfully complete tasks without experiencing sensory overwhelm
- Users trust the app enough to use it during moments of crisis
- Users' preferences persist reliably across sessions
- Users feel the app respects their privacy and autonomy
